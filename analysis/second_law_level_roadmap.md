# Second-Law-Level Roadmap for Structural Persistence

Status: internal roadmap after the admissible-maps / hierarchical-invariants
supplement.

This is not a new claim source, not validation evidence, and not a universal-law
declaration. Its purpose is to make the route to a second-law-level theory
explicit enough that the next work can be ordered without overclaim.

## 1. What "second-law-level" means here

This program should not try to copy thermodynamics literally. Structural
persistence does not have the same mathematical setting as the physical second
law: it does not start from a continuous symmetry group, a Lagrangian, or a
single physical entropy.

The realistic target is:

```text
A hierarchy of admissible-map invariance, covariance, defect identities,
typical nondecrease theorems, and limited-class universality theorems for
structural-maintenance problems.
```

In short:

```text
not one unconditional universal inequality,
but a family of law-like theorems generated by admissible structural maps.
```

The new supplement

```text
v2/補論_構造持続における許容写像と階層的不変量.md
```

is the mathematical spine for this route. It already fixes the most important
discipline:

- \(B_n\) is not claimed to be a single ordinary functor on all admissible maps.
- Iso maps preserve \(B_n\).
- Positive gauge changes make \(B_n\) covariant.
- Coarse-graining introduces saturation defect, not unconditional DPI.
- Proxy / estimation maps fall to frozen validation, not invariance theorems.
- Bernoulli-CSP is best read as a template-preserving generated class, not as a
  post-hoc collection of SAT-like examples.

### Component 2: total production / Sigma

Current status:

```text
stronger than expected; Lean core already exists
```

Existing Lean anchors:

- `Survival.TotalProduction`
- `Survival.ResourceBudgetToTotalProductionDrift`
- `Survival.ResourceBudgetToSigmaDrift`
- `Survival.CoarseTypicalNondecrease`
- `Survival.CoarseTotalProduction`
- `Survival.StochasticTotalProduction`

Already proven in Lean:

- \(\Sigma_n = B_n + C_n\) as cumulative total production;
- total production splits as contraction loss plus repair slack;
- repair slack is nonnegative under a repair-budget hypothesis;
- total production dominates cumulative contraction loss;
- exact-payment collapses total production back to cumulative loss;
- resource budget plus a contraction lower bound lifts to
  \(E[\Delta \Sigma_t] \ge \alpha\);
- if \(\alpha\ge 0\), expected cumulative total production is monotone;
- coarse expected cumulative total production is monotone under existing
  compatibility / resource-bounded / conditional-Azuma routes.

This means Component 2 is not a speculative future task. It is already mostly
formalized. The reader-facing wrapper layer now lives in
`Survival.SecondLawTotalProduction`. The remaining task is no longer the
mathematical core, but the stronger layers around it:

- explain that \(\Sigma\), not raw \(B_n\), is the natural open-system
  tendency candidate;
- keep expectation-level monotonicity separate from high-probability collapse.
- make the high-probability, admissible coarse-graining, and class-universality
  conditions explicit before claiming anything stronger than expectation-level
  tendency.

Do not claim yet:

```text
full second-law analogue
```

The current correct claim is:

```text
the expectation-level Sigma tendency schema is already formally accessible
through existing Lean theorems; the remaining work is mapping and wrapper
polish.
```

### Component 3: typical nondecrease

Current status:

```text
expectation-level schema mapped; high-probability schema remains conditional
```

Existing Lean anchors:

- `Survival.CoarseTypicalNondecrease`
- `Survival.ProbabilityConnection`
- `Survival.StochasticTotalProductionAzuma`
- `Survival.ResourceBoundedStochasticCollapse`
- `Survival.CoarseStochasticStoppingTimeCollapse`

What is already closed:

- nonnegative one-step total production implies monotone expected cumulative
  total production;
- resource-bounded or conditional-Azuma routes give the same expectation-level
  monotonicity under explicit assumptions;
- stopped-collapse / hitting-time statements exist under bounded-increment,
  margin, and concentration assumptions.

What remains:

- a reader-facing split between:
  - expectation-level tendency;
  - high-probability stopped collapse;
  - long-time / ergodic / almost-sure claims.

Do not merge these three.

The next theorem-wrapper target should say something like:

```text
Expectation-level typical nondecrease follows from nonnegative one-step Sigma
production, or from existing resource-bounded assumptions that imply it.
```

The high-probability version must keep the extra concentration and margin
assumptions.

### Component 5: cross-class unification

Current status:

```text
future work; do not front-run
```

This is the actual second-law-level target. It should only be attempted after
multiple limited-class universality theorems exist.

The eventual shape may be:

```text
For any admissible structural template class satisfying compatibility,
defect-control, and resource-cost lower-bound conditions, Sigma obeys the
appropriate expectation-level tendency theorem; class-specific collapse
statements then follow after margin and concentration assumptions.
```

This is not ready as a theorem today. It is a future synthesis target.

## 6. What not to do next

Do not immediately chase the final cross-class unification theorem. It is too
abstract until the limited classes are thicker.

Do not declare a physical second law analogue. The current best statement is:

```text
Structural persistence now has a concrete route toward a second-law-level
hierarchical unification, with Sigma tendency and admissible maps as the
mathematical spine.
```

Do not treat no-support empirical branches as failures of the formal kernel.
They remain mapping / proxy / domain-applicability outcomes unless they hit a
specification-fixed law-side theorem.

Do not collapse expectation-level monotonicity and high-probability collapse
into one claim. The latter needs margin and concentration assumptions.
